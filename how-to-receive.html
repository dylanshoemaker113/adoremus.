How to Receive

How Should I Approach Holy Communion?

Receiving the Eucharist is the center of Catholic life, but it's also surrounded by customs and expectations that can feel confusing, especially if you're new, returning after time away, or just visiting.

1

Who Can Receive?

Catholics

Yes — if two things are true:

  • 1. Baptized Catholic. You must be a baptized Catholic who has made your First Communion — whether that happened in childhood or as an adult through OCIA.
  • 2. State of grace. You can receive only when not conscious of a serious (mortal) sin that hasn't yet been confessed. If this is your case, go to Confession first (see "Returning to the Sacraments" below).
Before You Receive

The Eucharistic fast: fast from food and drink (except water and medicine) for at least one hour before Communion — a physical reminder that this isn't ordinary food, but really is Christ.

Non-Catholics

Not at this time. The Church asks that those who are not Catholic — including members of other Christian traditions — not receive Communion at Mass.

Why does the Church ask this?

Catholics believe that receiving Communion together is a sign of full, visible unity — not only about the Eucharist itself, but also about the unity of Catholic belief, and about being in communion with one's bishop and the Pope. The sign of unity Communion expresses isn't yet fully true between us, and that's what this practice acknowledges.

  • Visiting or not yet Catholic? You're warmly welcome to be present, to pray, and to make a "spiritual communion" (see Encounter Jesus and "If You Cannot Receive" below).
  • Interested in becoming Catholic? Find a Catholic parish near you and speak with the priest about their OCIA program.

What if I can't receive now?

2

Returning to the Sacraments

If you're a Catholic who has been away — whether for months, years, or decades — you are always welcome back.

If it has been a long time, an examination of conscience can help you prepare. Most parishes post regular confession times, and most priests are glad to help you through it if it's been a while.

You Will Be Welcomed, Not Interrogated

Whether it has been months, years, or decades since your last confession or Communion, the Church longs for your return and will welcome you back with open arms. The same Christ who is present in the Eucharist is the one waiting for you — not to shame you, but to welcome you home.

3

Receiving Reverently

The Church permits several legitimate ways of receiving Holy Communion, and it is the choice of the communicant to practice either option:

On the Tongue

The communicant opens their mouth and the priest or minister places the host directly on the tongue. This remains the traditional and universal norm in the Roman rite.

Posture when receiving on the tongue

Kneeling

Some people like to kneel to receive the Eucharist — a posture with a long history, expressing adoration of the One being received.

Standing

Others prefer to stand while receiving, often making a sign of reverence — such as a bow of the head or at the waist — just beforehand.

Where permitted

In the Hand

Also permitted in many places, the faithful are allowed to receive our Lord on their hands. To do so, the communicant forms a small “throne” with one hand beneath the other (not with one hand only), and the minister places the host in the palm. Once the host is placed on the palm, the communicant must receive it immediately. They must not walk away with the host, but rather consume it in directly in front of the minister. Please ensure that your hands are clean before reception, and that there are no remaining particles on your hand, not by wiping them off, but by carefully picking them up with your opposite hand and consuming them.

Different customs exist, but all should be done with great reverence for Our Lord. Whichever way you receive, the goal is to approach the Sacrament with the awareness that you are receiving God himself.

4

If You Cannot Receive

Spiritual Communion

If you can't receive Communion — whether you're not Catholic, not in a state of grace, or separated from the sacraments — you can make a "spiritual communion": an act of faith and longing, asking Jesus to come into your soul even though you can't receive Him sacramentally. See the Encounter Jesus page for a traditional prayer.

Crossed-Arms Blessing

If you choose to join the Communion procession, those who don't receive Communion — including children who haven't yet made their First Communion — may come forward with arms crossed over their chest to receive a blessing from the priest or deacon instead.

Remaining in Prayer

If you choose to stay in your pew during the Communion procession, you can use the time to pray or make a spiritual communion.

5

Thanksgiving After Communion

The minutes after receiving Communion are uniquely intimate — Christ is present within you in a way unlike any other moment. Rather than rushing out, many Catholics use this time for quiet thanksgiving.

Thank

Thank Jesus for coming to you in the Eucharist, and for allowing you to participate in the Heavenly Banquet. You can give thanks in your own words or by reciting a written prayer.

Ask

Bring to the Lord whatever is on your heart: your worries, your struggles, or your blessings.

Silence

Sometimes there are no words to describe how you feel. Simply siting in the presence of Christ is being in His Presence nonetheless.
