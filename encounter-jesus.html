Eucharistic Adoration

What It Is

Eucharistic Adoration is time spent in prayer before the Blessed Sacrament — either reserved in the tabernacle, or "exposed" for a period of adoration in a monstrance (sometimes at all hours of the day, in "perpetual adoration"). Simply put, it is time spent with Jesus.

Why Catholics Do It

If the Eucharist truly is Jesus — Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity — then He can be visited, spoken to, and adored just as the apostles could do during His earthly life. Adoration puts that belief into practice.

What Happens During Adoration

There's no fixed script. Some people come for a few minutes on the way to work, others commit to a regular weekly hour. Some pray with Scripture or devotionals, others sit in silence. The key is spending time with Christ, however that looks for you.

2

How to Make a Holy Hour

A "Holy Hour" is simply an extended time of prayer before the Blessed Sacrament. An hour can sound long, so here are some ways to use it.

Sit Silently

Simply being present is valid prayer. You can just sit with Christ, and you don't need to feel something for the time to "count."

Speak to Jesus

Talk to Him as you would a trusted friend — about your day, your worries, your gratitude, your struggles. He already knows, and He wants you to bring it to Him anyway.

Read Scripture

Bring a Bible and read slowly, especially in the Gospels. Let a phrase or image settle rather than rushing through chapters. As you read, listen to how the Lord is moving you while reading and what He is placing on your heart.

Pray the Rosary

The Rosary is a natural companion to adoration — meditating on the life of Christ while sitting in His actual presence.

Journal

Writing down what comes to mind — questions, gratitude, struggles, resolutions — can help a wandering mind settle and gives you something to look back on.

Spiritual Communion

What It Means

A "spiritual communion" is an act of faith, hope, and love — asking Jesus to come into your soul even when you cannot receive him sacramentally. It has been recommended by saints and spiritual writers for centuries: for those unable to receive Communion, or just anyone wanting to deepen their desire for Christ throughout the day.

Act of Spiritual Communion

My Jesus, I believe that You are present in the Most Holy Sacrament. I love You above all things, and I desire to receive You into my soul. Since I cannot now receive You sacramentally, come at least spiritually into my heart. I embrace You as if You were already there, and unite myself wholly to You. Never permit me to be separated from You. Amen.

— Traditional, after St. Alphonsus Liguori
